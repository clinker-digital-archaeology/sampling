## Part B - Survey Evaluation:

Identify and describe survey features:

1. Sample type: The 2018 General Social Survey on Giving, Volunteering and Participating (GSS-GVP) used a stratified probability sample. The design involved multi-stage sampling, with geographic stratification by province and selection of individuals within households.
2. Sample size: ~24,000 people completed the survey in 2018. 
3. Target population: Individuals aged 15 years and older living in private households in Canada’s ten provinces. 
4. Sampling frame: Based on Statistics Canada’s dwelling and telephone frames, including both landline and cellular telephone numbers to improve population coverage.
5. Survey mode(s): Mixed-mode adata collection, including computer-assisted telephone interviewing and an electronic self-administered online questionnaire. 
6. Timeline: Timeline:
Data collection took place between September and December 2018.
7. Response rate: The overall response rate was ~ 41.9%.
8. Weights: Survey weights were provided to adjust for unequal probabilities of selection and non-response. Bootstrap weights were also included to allow for accurate variance estimation.
9. Data processing: Responses were processed and compiled into a Public Use Microdata File (PUMF). Confidentiality protections were applied, and some variables were derived or grouped to reduce disclosure risk.
10. Cleaning, imputation, etc: Editing and imputation procedures were used to address inconsistent or blank responses. Income data were supplemented using administrative tax records where possible, and donor imputation methods were applied when necessary.
11. Sources of error: Sources of error include sampling error due to surveying a subset of the population, non-response error, and coverage error related to households without reliable telephone or internet access.
12. Limitations, known biases, etc: The exclusion of institutionalized individuals and residents of the territories. Non-response bias may also affect results if those who chose not to participate differ systematically from respondents. Changes in survey mode compared to earlier cycles may affect comparability over time.
